When should you move budget between campaigns?

Use a small worked example to compare the next dollar with the average you see today.

NOVEMBER 5, 2026

A low average cost per lead tells you what a campaign achieved with its current budget. It doesn’t tell you what another $500 will buy. Before shifting spend, ask what the next dollars are likely to add, and what the campaign losing budget will give up.

Work from comparable outcomes

Use the same qualification rule and a mature reporting period. Separate brand from non-brand when their roles differ. Check whether either campaign is limited by demand, geography, or the landing page before changing the budget.

Estimate the next dollars

Campaign A spends $1,000 for 10 qualified leads. Campaign B spends $1,000 for 8. A looks better on average. Suppose a recent $300 increase on A added one qualified lead, while the same increase on B added three. The observed marginal cost was $300 in A and $100 in B. That is a hypothesis for the next test, and a small sample can easily move either estimate.

Make the change measurable

  1. Write down the outcome and the maximum cost per qualified lead you can support.
  2. Change one budget allocation and log the date and reason.
  3. Wait for sales qualification, then compare incremental results and the volume you lost elsewhere.
