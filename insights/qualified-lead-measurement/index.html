How to measure what a qualified lead really costs

Connect ad spend to what sales accepts, and see which campaign deserves the next dollar.

NOVEMBER 5, 2026

When paid media and sales report different numbers, the first question is which event each team is counting. A form submission records a response. A qualified lead records that sales accepted the inquiry according to agreed criteria.

Agree on one qualification rule

Start with the conditions sales can check consistently. For example, an inquiry might need to come from a business in a supported market and include a use case your team can serve. Create separate statuses for accepted, disqualified, and pending so an incomplete review doesn’t look like a poor-quality lead.

Compare campaigns using the same lead cohort

Group leads by when the campaign acquired them. Use spend from that same period, then wait until enough leads have been reviewed to interpret the result. Track the open share next to cost per qualified lead so a campaign with a slower sales cycle gets a fair read.

Illustrative example: A campaign spends $1,200 and generates 40 leads. Eight qualify. The cost per submitted lead is $30, the cost per qualified lead is $150, and 20% of all leads have qualified. If 20 leads are still pending, 40% of resolved leads qualified. Both rates describe different things.

Validate the feedback before using it for bidding

Use a test lead to check that your source identifiers travel from ad click to form to CRM, then confirm the platform receives the intended event once. Compare platform receipt with CRM counts and investigate mismatches before making a new signal a bidding input. For Google Ads, enhanced conversions for leads can use first-party data to support offline conversion matching.

Platform reference: Google Ads Help on enhanced conversions for leads.
