How to test AI Max on a B2B Search campaign

Set up a test that tells you whether automation brings more of the leads you actually want.

NOVEMBER 5, 2026

Google’s AI Max for Search can expand query matching, customize ad text, and route visitors to other relevant pages when those features are enabled. Google has also announced more flexibility for testing with brand or location controls. For a B2B advertiser, the question is whether the extra reach produces sales-accepted demand at a cost the business can afford.

Start where you can read the result

Pick a campaign with a clearly defined offer, a focused landing page, and enough historical activity to establish a baseline. Pull spend, submitted leads, qualified leads, and the time sales usually takes to qualify them. If qualified outcomes arrive slowly or only a handful appear each month, set a longer evaluation window and define the result as directional.

Keep the test focused

  1. Write one hypothesis, such as “expanded search matching can find new enterprise buyers at a comparable cost per qualified lead.”
  2. Use a control and a treatment where available. Keep budget allocation, geography, and conversion definitions comparable. Record any feature settings that differ between the two arms.
  3. Before launch, review brand and location controls, negative keywords, URL restrictions, and which copy or pages automation can use. For an offer with distinct consumer and enterprise pages, review the landing page controls first.
  4. During the test, review spend on irrelevant terms, where users landed, and how qualification differs by query theme or geography.

Use the same decision rule for both arms

Compare qualified leads, cost per qualified lead, and the share of leads accepted by sales. Give the most recent leads time to mature. If the experiment brings more qualified leads but at a higher cost, compare the marginal cost with what the business can pay for incremental pipeline.

Before you launch: Write down the threshold for scaling and the amount of spend or irrelevant traffic that would end the test early.

Platform references: Google’s AI Max announcement and Google’s testing and planning controls.
