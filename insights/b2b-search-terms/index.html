PAID MEDIA

What your search terms say about lead quality

Separate buyer intent from research and consumer traffic before you change bids or budgets.

NOVEMBER 5, 2026

When leads slow down, it’s tempting to look at bids first. Start one layer closer to the customer. Pull the search terms, check what those people were actually asking for, and compare that intent with the landing page and the leads sales received.

Sort terms by what the searcher wants

For a business selling eSIM infrastructure, these are examples of how three similar-looking searches can represent different buyers.

Example queryLikely intentWhat to check
white label eSIM platformPotential business buyerDoes the page explain branding, onboarding, and next steps?
eSIM providersMixed or unclearDo search variants and CRM feedback support keeping it?
cheap eSIM for a holidayConsumer purchaseIs the query irrelevant to every offer this campaign promotes?

Follow the path from query to page

For each promising theme, compare the search term, headline, and landing page opening. A search for an API should reach a page that answers integration questions. A reseller search should explain who can resell, how onboarding works, and what support the partner receives. Both users can submit the same form, but they may need different reasons to submit it.

Review exclusions before applying them

Start with the clearly irrelevant terms that have material spend or recur frequently. Review each negative against the positive terms and adjacent offers you want to keep eligible. Record why it was added and check what changed in the next review. An ambiguous term with a thin sample is usually a candidate for monitoring.

A useful weekly view: Group spend by intent, flag where eligible buyers land, and record qualified outcomes where you can reliably connect them. Compare at the campaign or theme level when the CRM cannot support individual query attribution.
